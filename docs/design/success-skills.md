This is the second half of **Life** (`docs/design/life-skills.md`). Life now has two strands:
- **Living well**: body and health, feelings, staying safe, life online, home and everyday, getting around, our world,
  and the basics of getting on with people (`life-skills.md` §2).
- **Success**: the eleven skill families below: money, business, trading, communication, connections, negotiation,
  leadership, self-mastery, thinking, character and the future of work. The life-skills design's areas 6 (money) and 9
  (thinking and the future) move here and grow; area 3 keeps the basics (kindness, sharing, bullying).

Everything in `life-skills.md` holds here too: learn by doing (§3), easy means obvious (§10), parents see the learning
(§11), three tracks (Little 6-8, Middle 9-11, Big 12+), calm and safe (§6), English only, nothing personal kept or sent.

## 0. The short version

- **What success means in EasyCraft:** making good things happen for yourself *and for other people*. The EU's
  entrepreneurship framework defines it the same way: turning ideas into value for others, which can be financial,
  cultural or social [S2]. Money is a tool; trust, skills, health and friends are part of success too.
- **The evidence says which skills matter most:** self-control in childhood predicts adult health and wealth [S3]; social
  skills at five predict education, work and staying out of trouble [S4]; friendships across different groups predict
  getting ahead more than almost anything measured [S5]; employers' top skills are analytical thinking, resilience,
  leadership, creative thinking and self-awareness [S6]; simple if-then plans make goals much more likely [S7].
- **Teaching about money changes knowledge far more than behaviour** (+0.33 SD knowledge, +0.07 SD behaviour in school
  programmes [S1]). So the game makes children *do* it, again and again, with consequences that feel real and are safe:
  run a stand, then a shop, then a company; trade at a market where prices move for reasons you can see; save for a
  rocket; make deals; build a network of people who trust you.
- **The game's own world is the classroom:** gold earned by learning, the rocket as a big goal, building, the farm,
  buddies as a team, friends online: the Success strand turns these into a business and a life sandbox.
- **Guardrails:** never gambling (no random money rewards, no loot-box mechanics: they are linked to problem gambling,
  more so in teenagers [S9]); honesty always pays in the game's design; no real money, no real shares, no advice; no
  pressure tricks; trading with approved friends only.
- **First step:** L1's **My Money Stall** is built as the first step of **My Business**; then **Talk & Deal**, **The
  Connections Book** and **Success Stories** (S2), then the shop and the **Market Square** (S3), then teams, goals and
  habits and the Big track (S4).

## 1. The eleven families of success skills

Each family on three tracks. Built on EntreComp's three areas (ideas and opportunities, resources, into action: fifteen
competences [S2]), the OECD/INFE and EU financial frameworks (`life-skills.md` [L11]), CASEL and the WHO's life skills
(`life-skills.md` [L8], [L9]) and the WEF's skills outlook [S6].

| Family | Little (6-8) | Middle (9-11) | Big (12+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 Money mastery** | coins and notes, earning by helping, the saving jar, needs and wants, waiting for something better | a budget, saving for a goal (your rocket), comparing prices, the bank, interest ("the money tree"), giving | pay yourself first, compound growth over years, inflation, owning part of a company, spreading risk, good and bad debt, interest you pay, taxes and insurance (the idea) |
| **S2 Business** | a stand: make it, price it, sell it, say thank you, keep some to make more | a shop: ask customers what they want, make it, the price (what it costs + profit), a sign, reviews, a complaint fixed, profit and loss | a company: a supply chain, a loan, investors and a pitch, hiring helpers, cash flow, a rival, a business that also helps people |
| **S3 Trading and markets** | swapping fairly, why people trade | supply and demand (prices move for reasons you can see), buying low and selling high honestly, specialising and trading makes both richer | markets and exchanges, shares and dividends, diversifying, long-term versus short-term, **investing is not gambling**, scams that are too good to be true |
| **S4 Communication** | listen and say it back, ask nicely, say thank you and sorry | explain clearly (a buddy does exactly what you say), ask good questions, tell a story, write a clear message | speak to a group, pitch an idea, give and take feedback, body language and eye contact (VR), a phone or video call |
| **S5 Connections** | make a friend, remember names, help first | what people care about, being someone people can count on, introducing two people who can help each other, thanking | a network: mentors, people different from you [S5], keeping in touch, reputation, connections online done safely |
| **S6 Negotiation and persuasion** | fair turns, sharing deals | why does each person want it (interests), win-win, a fair offer, polite haggling, a promise is a deal | the walk-away option, persuading with honest reasons (never tricks), agreements and contracts (the idea), solving a dispute |
| **S7 Leadership and teamwork** | taking turns being the leader, helping the team | roles by strengths, a shared goal, encouraging, deciding together | delegating, a short meeting, handling disagreement, taking responsibility, leading by example |
| **S8 Self-mastery** | wait for the bigger reward, finish what you start, try again | goals in steps, "when X happens, I will do Y" plans [S7], a small daily habit, planning the day | habits that stick, focus and time (big things first), self-control [S3], bouncing back after failure, knowing your strengths |
| **S9 Thinking for success** | "what if", lots of ideas | solving problems step by step, choices and what happens next, checking if something is true | analytical thinking (the WEF's top skill [S6]), risk and chance done honestly, creative thinking, learning how to learn, what AI is and how to use and check it |
| **S10 Character and trust** | honesty, keeping your word, fairness | why trust is worth more than a quick win, giving back, gratitude | integrity when nobody watches, courage to do right, responsibility, the long game |
| **S11 Work and the future** | jobs people do (Job Days) | a job or a business, skills that every job needs, stories of how people started | the future of work [S6], a portfolio of what you have made, interviews, choosing what to learn next |

## 2. How the game teaches success: the world is the sandbox

Lessons alone barely change behaviour [S1]; simulations that let children run things raise their knowledge (JA BizTown's
evaluation: 8.95 to 13.73 on its test [S8]). EasyCraft can go further, because the child already lives in a world with
gold, goals, a farm, building, buddies and friends. Seven signature systems, each a place in the world and a set of
Experiences (the Experience loop of `life-skills.md` §3):

1. **My Business** (S1, S2, S4, S10): the long-running heart, from a stand to a company in the child's own world.
   - *Little:* a stand. Make lemonade or bread (the farm's wheat), set a price, serve buddy customers, count the coins,
     keep some to buy more lemons, put some in the saving jar.
   - *Middle:* a shop the child builds. Customers with their own wishes, budgets and moods; ask them what they want; make
     it; price it (what it cost + profit); a sign; stars and reviews; a complaint listened to and fixed; the shop's
     ledger (money in, money out, profit) in pictures; a buddy helper paid a wage; a rival shop that wins customers back
     only with better quality or service, never tricks.
   - *Big:* a company. A supply chain (farm → mill → bakery → shops), a bank loan for a factory (its interest), investors
     who get a share and dividends after a pitch, a late payment and cash flow, a second shop in another world (the
     cosmos), a share of the profit for the village (a social enterprise).
   - The business pays real gold, **capped like learning's gold** (the server's limits) and **only while the child plays
     it** (customers come while you are there): learning by doing funds the rocket, and it can never be left running to
     farm gold.
2. **The Market Square** (S3, S6, S9): a market where goods' prices move by supply and demand with **causes you can see**
   (a dry season: wheat goes up; a festival: cakes go up; many sellers: the price falls), told on the Price Board. Buy,
   sell, specialise, trade with buddy merchants; haggle politely. *Big:* own shares in buddy businesses and get their
   dividends; spread your eggs across baskets (a storm hits one kind of business); over many seasons the game shows,
   honestly, that patient owning beats jumping in and out. A lesson with dice shows why gambling loses over time.
3. **The Connections Book** (S5, S4, S10): the people of the worlds have names, jobs, wishes and needs (the baker needs
   flour, the farmer needs tools, the engineer needs a buyer). The child meets them, remembers them in the book, helps
   first, keeps promises (delivers on time), introduces people who can help each other, and is trusted more for it:
   new chances open ("the baker tells you about a market in Mars's dome"). Mentors teach a skill when asked politely.
   Trust shows as the people's warmth to you, never as a public score that shames.
4. **Talk & Deal** (S4, S6): practice conversations with characters, the child choosing what to say (pictures on the
   Little track, phrases later; no typing, no voice recorded): a customer's complaint (listen, say sorry, fix it), a
   deal with the farmer (find what both want), a pitch to friendly investors (in VR, standing in front of them: the
   problem, the idea, why you, what you need), instructions to a buddy who does *exactly* what you said (clear words
   matter).
5. **Team Projects** (S7): a project board (the goal, the roles, the tasks, the days) for a big build or the rocket;
   leading buddies and robots with different strengths; with friends online, a leader who changes each project.
6. **Goals & Habits** (S8): the Goal Board (a dream → a goal → steps → if-then plans: "When I finish a lesson, I put
   10 gold in my rocket jar" [S7]); the habit garden (a plant grows on each day you keep one small good habit; a missed
   day never kills it: gentle, never guilt); waiting for the bigger reward (the bank's interest, the rocket) as the
   game's own rhythm.
7. **Success Stories** (S11, S10, S8): short read-aloud stories and Shorts of real people who built things: how they
   started small, failed and tried again, helped others; inventors, nurses, teachers, farmers, engineers and founders,
   from many countries; every fact checked with its source; "Which skill did they use?"

**In VR** (`life-skills.md` §4): standing at your shop's counter handing over goods and counting change; looking an
investor in the eye during a pitch; a friendly audience for a talk; shaking hands on a deal; leading a team in one room.

## 3. Guardrails: success the right way

- **Value for others.** Every business mission is about making something people want and treating them well; the
  simulation rewards quality, honesty and service over time, never cheating, tricks or exploiting buddies.
- **Never gambling.** No random money rewards, no loot boxes, no spins, no near-misses, no betting, anywhere [S9]. Markets
  move for reasons the child can see. The difference between investing and gambling is taught on purpose.
- **No real money, no real shares, no advice.** Everything is pretend or game gold; the Big track teaches ideas and says
  "talk to a grown-up you trust" for real money.
- **No pressure tricks.** No countdown timers to buy, no fear of missing out, no streak guilt; customers wait kindly.
- **Online trading only with approved friends** (the existing friend approvals), game gold only, checked by the server,
  limited, and reportable.
- **Success means many things.** Stories and missions show many kinds of success; no worship of wealth; generosity and
  giving back are part of every track.
- **Facts checked, experts asked.** Money and business content reviewed by a financial educator before it ships (the
  owner arranges; the lead prepares the review sheet), like the first aid review.

## 4. How parents see it (`life-skills.md` §11)

The business ledger and the Goal Board are evidence a parent can read: "Your child ran a shop for three days: 120 gold
profit, saved 40%, fixed two complaints kindly"; "Set a goal (the rocket to Mars), planned the steps, and kept a habit
for 12 days". Before-and-after checks for the ideas (needs and wants, profit, supply and demand, win-win).
