## Rulings (decide and write down; cost if wrong)
1. **Memory first, naming grade 3, 4, 5 or 6; "Try it in the world: … Next: …" last** (the test enforces both). Some
   "try it" ideas are for the real world with a grown-up (the Pole Star, dropping two balls). *Cost:* none.
2. **Hard history is named, never shown, and marked in the outline** (research §5.4 rule 3, §5.5 ruling 9): voyages
   ("voyages that began conquest, Magellan's death"), encounters in the Americas ("the conquest of the Americas,
   disease, forced labour"), the Ottoman Empire ("the taking of Constantinople, wars of conquest"), monarchs and
   parliaments ("the Civil War, Charles I put to death in 1649"), trading companies ("the Dutch conquest of the Banda
   Islands"). No semicolons inside a line: the grown-ups' page joins the units with "; ". Words: "died", "lost their
   lives", "put to death"; the test bans gruesome words (and "killed", "murder") in the lessons and the generators.
   Geography's disaster units are not marked (natural hazards, taught since grade 4; deaths are named, not counted).
   *Cost:* a line on the grown-ups' page for hazards if the owner wants one.
3. **Encounters told with respect:** the peoples first, the exchange both ways, disease as a disaster, conquest with
   the Indigenous allies who fought the Aztecs, forced labour and de las Casas, resistance and living languages, and
   the slave trade left to grade 8's unit (named only). *Cost:* none.
4. **Religion as belief and culture:** Luther's "new beliefs about the Church", Henry VIII's break with Rome as a
   marriage and power dispute, the Ottomans' faith communities, Akbar's debates, three faiths beginning in the Middle
   East, the Hajj, Galileo's trial ("Church leaders of the time disagreed"), the golden rule "in many faiths and
   traditions". *Cost:* none.
5. **Politics in general terms:** the UK Parliament is the worked example of law-making, with the US veto as a second
   system; no parties (the test also bans "whigs"); the Civil War told as a struggle over who holds power; UK facts are
   said to be the UK's (petitions, Gift Aid, credit cards at 18, the Youth Parliament). *Cost:* other countries' stages.
6. **No disputed places or names:** the Middle East is described by its seas ("the Gulf", never Persian or Arabian),
   mountains, deserts, rivers and undisputed capitals, with no list of countries; Russia by its land, cities and
   climate only. The test bans a list of disputed names. *Cost:* no country list for the Middle East.
7. **Maps have no coastlines** (grade 5's ruling), so the Middle East and Russia use `voyage_map`'s zoom window, not
   grade 5's whole-world `latlong`. *Cost:* coastline outlines later.
8. **Numbers as the sources give them, rounded for children:** Zheng He "hundreds of ships and about 28,000 people"
   (sources give 62-317 ships); "perhaps 180" Bibles, 49 surviving; the Taj Mahal "about 20,000 workers … finished by

9. **Money is gold:** spices', shares', sea walls', stalls' and cards' numbers are made up in gold; Gift Aid is "1 gold

10. **Kepler's third law** as "years² = distance³" with whole squares and cubes only; **energy up a food chain** as
    "about a tenth"; **magnitudes**: 10 times the shaking a step, about 32 times the energy, two steps "about 1,000"

11. **Names in the font's letters:** "arete" (no circumflex), "Süleymaniye" and "Cortés" (ü and é are drawn), "Taino",
    "Guarani", "Topkapi"; "Hernán" left out. *Cost:* none.

14. **voyage_map's place names may have 14 letters** (Yekaterinburg), since they are placed where they are clear.
