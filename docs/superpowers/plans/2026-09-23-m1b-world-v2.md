# M1b "World v2" Implementation Plan — underground, water, realistic graphics, double jump, animals, sleep, weapons, chat commands

> **For agentic workers:** executed natively by the plan author (single godot-mcp editor client, shared emulator). Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax. TDD for every rule in `core/`; engine glue verified via godot-mcp and the emulator.

**Goal:** Make the world feel alive like Minecraft and beyond: real underground systems, real water (oceans, rivers, flowing water, underwater view), a switchable realistic graphics style, and a double/triple jump option.

**Architecture:** Generator work stays pure-rule first: new shapes (`CaveShape`, water/biome rules, ore placement) are deterministic functions of world coordinates and the seed, unit-tested, then written into voxel buffers by `MeadowsGenerator` on worker threads. Cave carving uses Minecraft's trick: sample noise on a coarse 4-block grid, interpolate, and skip every 4×4×4 cell whose corners prove no cave can pass. Flowing water is a host-side, bounded, tick-based simulation. The graphics style swaps textures on the shared block materials (same atlas layout), so switching is instant with no remeshing.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-23-realm-hoppers-design.md` §3b (added with this plan).

## Global constraints
- Everything from the M1 plan still applies (Godot 4.7.2, typed GDScript, landscape, emulator = test device, host-validated edits).
- World vertical range stays −64…192; **bedrock** at the bottom is unbreakable and not placeable.
- Generation must not regress acceptance: ≥ 30 FPS average, no frame > 100 ms while travelling 2 km (autopilot), in **both** graphics styles.
- New textures must be CC0 (ambientCG, Poly Haven) and credited in `assets/CREDITS.md`.
- Water never floats over carved caves: no cave carving within 6 blocks below any water surface.

## Review focus
1. Digging down anywhere must never drop the player out of the world (bedrock floor, unbreakable, also for stamps, mirror and batches).
2. Flowing water must stay bounded (no runaway spreading, no frame spikes) and never flood a player's build endlessly.
3. Chunk borders: caves, rivers and ores are functions of world coordinates only — no seams between chunks.
4. Style switching mid-game keeps every block's look consistent (same tile layout) and persists across restarts.
5. Performance on the emulator in the realistic style (bigger textures, glow, sky) and with 20 animals around.
6. Animals in multiplayer: only the host simulates and spawns them; pets belong to exactly one player.
7. Sleep in multiplayer: one sleeping player never skips the night for everyone; leaving the game while asleep cannot block the skip forever.
8. Weapons in multiplayer: the host validates every hit (reach, cooldown); a client cannot hurt or fling what it cannot reach, and tool powers obey the same edit rules as building.
9. Chat: a client cannot run host-only commands, spam, or bypass the age-band chat rules by sending raw RPCs.
